How this was measured

Each pair below is a real frame from the game, captured by driving it with genuine pointer events in headless Chrome — dragging items, tapping answers — and freezing the exact moment. The left frame runs commit 3a70022, the build as it was reported. The right frame runs the current branch. Nothing is mocked up: same harness, same window size, same level, only the code differs.

Timings come from the same runs, and the voice-over positions come from decoding the instruction clip and reading its energy envelope, so “when the voice says down” is a measurement rather than a guess.

BUG-1

Hand hint took too long to appear

Fixed

“The hand gesture visual hint on the ‘Next’ button takes approximately 5 to 6 seconds of inactivity to appear. For young learners, this long delay causes confusion and idle waiting.”

As reported

The real figure was worse than reported: 10.02 s. All seven levels ship a single arrowDelaySeconds of 10, and that one value gated every hint a level has — the hand on Next, the hand on Try Again, the hand on the correct item, and the ghost drag demo.

The drag demo was the worst of them. Armed at 10 s, a child has normally dropped the item long before it is due, so in practice it never appeared at all — and the one child who needed it sat waiting ten seconds.

BUG-2

“Lighter” arrow lagged the narration

Fixed

“The cyan ‘Lighter’ arrow indicator does not render when the narration speaks ‘light things…’, appearing only after a noticeable delay near the end of the voice line.”

As reported

Both arrows were cued off the instruction text as it typed. But the text types at a flat rate while speech is not flat — this line has a 0.61 s pause on the comma and 0.62 s of silence at the end. Decoding the clip shows that neither arrow ever coincided with its own word: the cyan one fired at 5.00 s, after “up” had finished, in the trailing silence; the red one at 2.44 s, inside the silent comma pause.

Both are now timed to the recording itself, and the line is typed word-by-word across the spans it is actually spoken over — so the sentence pauses where the voice pauses.

BUG-3

Level 3 called a toy bus “the bus”

Fixed, text and voice

“Referring to the toy object simply as a ‘bus’ creates logical confusion in an educational context, as a real bus is vastly heavier than a cricket ball.”

As reported

Agreed, and it undercuts the lesson: a balance tipping toward the cricket ball reads as nonsense to a child picturing a real vehicle. The artwork is unmistakably a toy, so the copy was what was wrong. Both of the level's lines are corrected, verified by capturing what the banner actually renders rather than the source string.

The narration says it too. Not re-performed — re-cut. The same actor already says “toy” in this game, in Let_us_place_the_toy_car_on_the_balance and Here_is_a_Pencil_and_a_Toy_car, recorded in the same session. Both are the same construction — “the toy X on the balance” — so the intonation transfers and the timbre matches by construction rather than by imitation.

Only the speech is taken from the source; the silence either side comes from the target, so the room tone under the sentence never changes — that, not the seam, is what usually gives a splice away. Cuts land on zero crossings, levels are matched sentence-to-sentence so the word keeps its prominence inside the phrase, and the gap after it reproduces the 100 ms the actor leaves before “car”.

The two re-cut lines
FileWasNow“toy” lands atPeak
Here_is_a_ball_and_a_toy_bus.wav3.673 s4.146 s2.70–3.07 s0.990

The first one’s level match pushed it to 1.111 — clipping. Rather than squash the word, the whole line is trimmed 1.00 dB: the sentence keeps its internal balance exactly and loses a decibel against its neighbours, which is nothing.

The seams measure inaudible. The largest sample-to-sample step at either join is 0.011–0.021, against 0.35–0.59 for the loudest step in the same file. A seam three to five percent the size of steps the recording already contains cannot be picked out. Typing pace barely moved either — 143.0 and 109.8 ms per character against 146.9 and 111.1 — so the lines still finish with the voice.

BUG-5

Tapping the title background triggered the voice-over

Fixed

“In the first screen the background looks tappable — when I tap the title, the voice-over starts.”

As reported in session

The splash backdrop is a full-screen button, and its only job was to play the title line. Two things followed on a screen whose one real control is Let’s Go: the whole picture took a pointer cursor, and pressing anywhere dimmed the entire splash. Every tap also restarted the line.

The backdrop is now inert, and the title line has a control of its own instead: a speaker button, top-left, clear of both the artwork and Let’s Go. The line plays on arrival where the platform permits it, and the button is there for a child who wants it again — or who is on a browser that blocked it.

Autoplay is a platform setting

No browser plays audio before the user has interacted with the page, and no code gets around that. Where the game ships it will simply autoplay — set setMediaPlaybackRequiresUserGesture(false) on an Android WebView, allow="autoplay" on an iframe embed, or mediaTypesRequiringUserActionForPlayback = [] on iOS.

BUG-6

Actions had no sound, and buttons had no press

Fixed

“Add interaction sounds where they’re missing — tap, drag, drop and more — make buttons feel tappable and pressed like a real game, and show the hand cursor only on things you can actually interact with.”

As requested

Three sounds existed and all three marked a result. Every action was silent — including a wrong answer, which meant right and wrong were distinguishable only by reading. Six cues were added, synthesised like the existing ones so there are no new files to load. Each is detuned slightly on every fire: the same sample twice running is what makes a game sound mechanical, and children hear it before adults do.

BUG-7

Next and Try Again sat still, and hints interrupted

Fixed

“Make Try Again and Next look tappable and pressed, add a pulse animation, and give a hand hint if the child is idle for 3–5 seconds.”

As requested

When either button appears it is the only thing left to do, and both sat as still as the table. They now breathe until they are used, with the shadow travelling with the scale so the button reads as lifting off the table rather than merely growing.

The hint delay also moved off the wrong clock. It counted from the moment a phase opened, so a child four seconds into dragging an item — not idle — got a hand on screen while they were still working. It now counts from whichever came later, the hint being armed or the child’s last action.

Both halves are needed. Without the arming time, a child who has spent fifteen seconds listening to an instruction is already “idle” when the next phase opens, and the hand lands on the same frame the item becomes draggable — measured at 0.04 s while building this.

BUG-8

The finish screen had nothing to tap

Fixed

“Create a next button on the game end screen, bottom-right, with a looping outer animation, a smooth pulse, and a hand hint after 3 seconds idle.”

As requested

Checking the end screen first turned this from a polish request into a bug. Driving the game through all seven levels and auditing what a child can touch there returned “interactable controls: NONE” — the panel is a single image with no children. A child who completed the whole game was stranded on that screen permanently, which is the worst place in the game to be stuck.

Level 7’s own Next button gets a second life rather than a new control being invented — it is already wired, sized, and already takes the press, the cursor and the hand hint. Three things were needed: reparent it into the panel, which is the last sibling and was covering it; park it bottom-right; and raise Image.color.a, which is authored at 0. In the original this is the last level and the button is never shown, so it was left invisible — the first attempt placed a fully transparent button, and the ring and the hand appeared over nothing at all. Diagnosed from the computed alpha, not guessed.

The ring runs on ::after, the breathe on ::before, and the press on the element’s own scale — three animations on three properties. A CSS animation outranks a normal declaration, so sharing one property would have meant a pulsing button that could not be pressed.

Nothing follows this game, so tapping it starts round two. That is one line if the host app should be handed control instead.

Found on the way, not reported

Four things nobody had filed, each of which would have reached a child:

  • The finish screen was a dead end — see BUG-8 above. Complete the game and you were stuck there for good.
  • The drag demo could never come back. It is retired the moment a drag begins and was never re-armed on a drag that ended without a drop — so the one child who most needed it, the one who picked the item up and could not work out where it goes, was the only child who could never see it again.
  • Level 7’s Next button is authored invisible (alpha 0), which only surfaced because something finally needed to show it.
  • A wrong answer had no sound at all, so right and wrong were distinguishable only by reading — on a screen built for children who mostly cannot.

What this pass does not cover

Stated plainly, because a report that only lists successes is not much use:

  • I have never heard this game. Verification runs muted in headless Chrome. Every cue is confirmed to fire, in the right order, at the right moment — and the spliced voice lines measure clean — but whether any of it sounds right is a judgement only a person with speakers can make. That is the one open item worth your time.
  • Synthetic taps are not trusted gestures. Where a browser blocks autoplay, the once-only logic is proven but the audio itself cannot be unlocked headlessly.
  • The re-cut lines are a splice, not a performance. The word is the same actor from the same session and the seams measure inaudible, but it was assembled, not spoken. A real re-record would still be better.
  • Those two files are WAV, 389 and 412 KB against 37 and 42 KB of Ogg Vorbis, because nothing available here can encode Vorbis. One ffmpeg -c:a libvorbis pass takes them under 45 KB; only the two extensions in AUDIO_FIXES would change.
  • The instruction-7 word timings are tied to the current recording. If that line is re-recorded or translated, the copy falls back to flat typing rather than mistiming itself — but the spans would want re-measuring.
